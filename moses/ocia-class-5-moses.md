## Slide 3 — The Map of the Story: The Five Books of Moses (The Torah / Pentateuch) (5–8 min)

- The five books traditionally **associated with** Moses, known as the **Torah** (Hebrew: "teaching" or "law") or the **Pentateuch** (Greek: "five books") — the foundational section of the Old Testament
- Today's class lives mostly in **Exodus**

*(The table below is optional detail — if short on time, name the five books in one sentence each.)*

| Book | Name means | What it contains | Role in the story |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Genesis** | "origin" | Creation, fall, flood, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph | God's **promise** to the patriarchs |
| **Exodus** | "departure" | Slavery in Egypt, Moses, Passover, Red Sea, Sinai | God's **redemption** of his people |
| **Leviticus** | "concerning the Levites (priests)" | Sacrifices, priesthood, worship, holiness laws | How the redeemed **worship and live** with God |
| **Numbers** | "countings" (two censuses) | Forty years wandering in the desert | The **journey** — Israel's trials, God's faithfulness |
| **Deuteronomy** | "second law" | Moses' farewell sermons, renewal of the covenant, the Shema | **Covenant renewed** for the new generation |

**One continuous story:** promise → redemption → worship → journey → renewal — the Pentateuch ends with Israel poised to enter the Promised Land, leaving the story open to its continuing fulfillment
- Jesus read the Torah as speaking of himself: "Everything written about me in the law of Moses and in the prophets and psalms must be fulfilled" (Lk 24:44)

**🖼️ Image hint:** An open ancient Torah scroll on a wooden table in warm light, with five softly glowing medallion illustrations hovering above it: a starry sky (Genesis), a parted sea (Exodus), an altar with rising incense (Leviticus), a desert camp under stars (Numbers), and a teacher gathered with his people (Deuteronomy) — gentle, luminous.

## Slide 4 — The Long Night of Egypt: A Nation Waits for Its Deliverer (8–11 min)

- After Joseph saved Egypt from famine, his brothers and their families settled there — about **seventy persons** in all (Gen 46:26-27)
- In Egypt, "the Israelites were fertile and prolific; they multiplied" (Ex 1:7) — from one family to a nation
- Then "a new king, who knew nothing of Joseph, rose over Egypt" (Ex 1:8) — fear turned to oppression: forced labor, brick-making, and finally the killing of Hebrew baby boys (Ex 1:9-22)
- Through it all, God had not forgotten his promise: long before, he had told Abraham his descendants would be enslaved — but that he would bring them out with great wealth (Gen 15:13-14)
- "The Israelites groaned in their slavery and cried out... God heard their groaning and was mindful of his covenant with Abraham" (Ex 2:23-25)

**When did this happen?** The Exodus is generally placed in the second millennium B.C.; its precise historical date is debated.

**Key teaching:** God's silence does not mean God's absence — across the long centuries he remained faithful to his promise, and he raises up a deliverer **in his time**.

**🖼️ Image hint:** A quiet, dignified scene: Hebrew workers shaping bricks under a heavy Egyptian sky, the pyramids hazy in the distance — while a single soft ray of light falls on an elderly laborer kneeling in prayer, his face peaceful — sorrow and hope together, subdued tones.

## Slide 5 — Who Was Moses? (Exodus 2) (11–13 min)

- Born a Hebrew in Egypt when Pharaoh ordered Hebrew boys killed (Ex 1:22–2:10)
- Rescued from the Nile, raised in Pharaoh's own household (Ex 2:5-10)
- As an adult, Moses acts violently, kills an Egyptian, and flees to Midian (Ex 2:11-15)
- Forty years as a shepherd in the desert — God was preparing him

**Discussion starter:** Moses' past seemed, from a human perspective, to disqualify him. Why would God choose such a man — and make him leader, prophet, lawgiver, and intercessor?

**🖼️ Image hint:** A moonlit Nile scene: a small woven basket resting among tall reeds, gentle ripples around it, warm torchlight from the distant Egyptian palace across the water, and the baby sleeping peacefully with a soft glow of moonlight on the basket — tender and calm.

## Slide 6 — The Burning Bush: God Takes the Initiative (Exodus 3:1-10) (13–16 min)

- While tending sheep at Horeb, the mountain of God, Moses sees a bush burning but not consumed (Ex 3:1-3)
- God calls him by name: "Moses! Moses!" — and Moses answers, "Here I am" (Ex 3:4)
- God reveals himself: "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" (Ex 3:6)
- God has seen the affliction of his people and will set them free — **through Moses**

> "Here again the initiative is God's. From the midst of the burning bush he calls Moses." — CCC 2575

**🖼️ Image hint:** A quiet desert night: an elderly shepherd kneeling barefoot, his sandals set aside, before a lone thorn bush wrapped in gentle golden-white flame that does not consume it — soft light on Moses' upturned face, a few sheep resting nearby — wonder and peace.

## Slide 7 — The Name of God: I AM WHO AM (Exodus 3:13-15) (16–18 min)

- God reveals his name as **"I AM WHO AM"** (Ex 3:14), expressing his living, faithful, and incomparable presence
- The Hebrew divine name (YHWH) is associated with God's living and mysterious presence — not exhausted by any one philosophical interpretation
- A fundamental revelation for both the Old and the New Covenants (cf. CCC 203-204, 280)

**Connection to look for later:** Jesus speaks this name of himself — "Before Abraham was, I AM" (Jn 8:58)

**🖼️ Image hint:** A serene mountain landscape at first light: soft clouds parting around the peak, and a column of pure warm light descending onto the mountainside, with the faint Hebrew letters of the divine name glowing gently within the light — quiet majesty, no human figure.

## Slide 8 — The Exodus: God Frees His People (Exodus 12–15) (18–21 min)

- The plagues reveal the LORD's supremacy over Pharaoh and the powers Egypt trusted (Ex 7–12)
- The **Passover**: the blood of the lamb saves Israel from death (Ex 12)
- Crossing of the Red Sea: from slavery to freedom, from death to life (Ex 14)
- Israel becomes a people — freed **by God's mighty hand**, not by their own strength (cf. CCC 62, 72; see slide 2)

> "I am the LORD. I will free you from the forced labor of the Egyptians and rescue you from their slavery." — Exodus 6:6

**🖼️ Image hint:** A wide, awe-inspiring view of the crossing: a corridor of dry seabed between two still walls of water, families with children, carts, and flocks walking through in a long procession, a soft pillar of cloud glowing overhead — quiet wonder, gentle blue-green and gold tones.

## Slide 9 — The Covenant at Sinai and the Great Commandment (Exodus 19–20; Deuteronomy 6) (21–28 min)

- At Mount Sinai, God makes a **covenant**: "You shall be to me a kingdom of priests, a holy nation" (Ex 19:5-6)
- God does not free Israel merely from slavery — he frees them **for covenant communion and worship**
- God gives the **Decalogue** — the Ten Commandments — through Moses (Ex 20:1-17)
- The Law is holy and good, given within a covenant relationship, and preparing Israel for Christ (cf. CCC 62, 1961-1964)
- Jesus called the words below **"the greatest and the first commandment"** (Mt 22:37-38)

### 📖 The Shema — Please Read Aloud Together (Deuteronomy 6:4-9, NABRE)

> **4** Hear, O Israel! The LORD is our God, the LORD alone!
> **5** Therefore, you shall love the LORD, your God, with your whole heart, and with your whole being, and with your whole strength.
> **6** Take to heart these words which I command you today.
> **7** Keep repeating them to your children. Recite them when you are at home and when you are away, when you lie down and when you get up.
> **8** Bind them on your arm as a sign and let them be as a pendant on your forehead.
> **9** Write them on the doorposts of your houses and on your gates.

**Reflection questions after reading:**
- This passage is called the *Shema* ("Hear!") — the basic principle of the whole Mosaic law: since the Lord alone is God, Israel must love him with an undivided heart
- How would your life change if you repeated these words "when you lie down and when you get up"?
- Jesus cites this exact passage as the greatest commandment (Mt 22:37-38; Mk 12:29-30; Lk 10:27)

**🖼️ Image hint:** Moses on the mountain slope holding the two stone tablets, a soft mantle of cloud and warm light around the summit, and below at the foot of the mountain the people gathered quietly, faces lifted up — reverent and orderly, golden light throughout.

## Slide 10 — God Teaches His People to Worship: The Tabernacle and the Ark of the Covenant (Exodus 25–31, 40) (28–33 min)

- After the covenant at Sinai, God gives Moses **detailed instructions for worship**: God initiates and teaches Israel's worship; Israel responds through sacrifice, prayer, and obedience (Ex 25–31)
- "They shall make a sanctuary for me, that I may dwell in their midst" (Ex 25:8)
- **The Tabernacle** (tent of meeting): a portable sanctuary at the very center of Israel's camp — God dwelling among his people
- **The Ark of the Covenant**: a chest of acacia wood overlaid with pure gold, crowned by two golden cherubim (Ex 25:10-22)
  - Hebrews 9:4 associates the Ark with the tablets of the covenant, a jar of manna, and Aaron's rod (Old Testament passages emphasize the tablets — cf. 1 Kgs 8:9)
  - Its golden lid, the "mercy seat," was God's throne — the place where God met and spoke with his people
- God's **glory cloud** filled the tabernacle, and it guided Israel through the desert (Ex 40:34-38)
- With it came the **priesthood of Aaron, sacrifices, the Sabbath, and liturgical feasts** — a whole way of approaching the living God

*(Architectural details are optional — if short on time, keep the key teaching and move on.)*

**Key teaching:** God does not only free his people from slavery — he teaches them **how to live in his presence and worship him**.

**🖼️ Image hint:** The Holy of Holies in warm candlelight: the golden Ark with two cherubim spreading their wings over the mercy seat, a soft column of light resting on the lid, gentle incense curling through the beams, the surrounding curtains in deep shadow — sacred, hushed, beautiful.

## Slide 11 — From Israel's Liturgy to Ours (33–35 min)

> "The Church's liturgy has retained certain elements of the worship of the Old Covenant as integral and irreplaceable, adopting them as her own: notably, reading the Old Testament; praying the Psalms; above all, recalling the saving events and significant realities which have found their fulfillment in the mystery of Christ." — CCC 1093

- The **cloud and the crossing of the Red Sea** prefigured Baptism; **water from the rock** and **manna in the desert** prefigured the gifts of Christ and the Eucharist, "the true bread from heaven" (CCC 1094)
- The **Eucharist fulfills the Jewish Passover** — Jesus gave the Passover its definitive meaning at the Last Supper (CCC 1340)
- At the **Easter Vigil**, the Church re-reads and re-lives these great events of salvation history — including the Exodus — in the "today" of her liturgy (CCC 1095); this is the liturgical setting in which many catechumens receive Baptism
- In Catholic typology, **Mary** fulfills the Ark's role as the dwelling place of God's presence, because she bore the incarnate Son: she is "the daughter of Zion in person, the ark of the covenant, the place where the glory of the Lord dwells" (CCC 2676)

**🖼️ Image hint:** A gentle side-by-side composition labeled "Old Covenant worship and its fulfillment in the Eucharist": on the left, priests in white linen carrying the golden Ark through the desert; on the right, a priest elevating the host at a candlelit altar — the same soft light linking both scenes across the frame — quiet continuity.

## Slide 12 — Moses the Intercessor: The Golden Calf *(optional — summarize briefly if short on time)* (35–37 min)

- While Moses is on the mountain, the people make a golden calf (Ex 32:1-6)
- Moses pleads with God for the people; God spares them (Ex 32:11-14)
- Moses asks to see God's glory: "The LORD, the LORD, a merciful and gracious God" (Ex 34:6)
- Moses' intercession does not compete with Christ's unique mediation — it foreshadows it and depends upon it (cf. CCC 2574; Pope Francis presents Moses as an intercessor who stands between God and his people, General Audience, June 17, 2020)

**🖼️ Image hint:** A somber, quiet scene: Moses descending the mountain path holding the tablets, pausing as he looks down at the camp below where people dance around a golden calf lit by firelight — Moses' face full of sorrow, one hand raised gently toward heaven — muted evening tones.

## Slide 13 — Forty Years in the Wilderness (Numbers 13–14; Deuteronomy 8) (37–40 min)

- At the border of Canaan, **twelve spies** scout the land; ten bring back a fearful report, and the people **refuse to enter** the land God promised (Num 13–14)
- God's judgment: **forty years of wandering** — until that unbelieving generation passes away (Num 14:33-34)
- Yet God never abandons his people in the desert: **manna** each morning, **water from the rock**, clothes that did not wear out (Deut 8:3-4)
- The wilderness had a purpose: "to test you, to know what was in your heart" (Deut 8:2)
- "Not by bread alone does one live, but by every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD" (Deut 8:3) — the very verse Jesus quoted when tempted in the desert (Mt 4:4)
- The **bronze serpent** lifted up on a pole — all who looked upon it lived (Num 21:9) — the sign Jesus applied to his own crucifixion: "so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (Jn 3:14)

**Key teaching:** The wilderness is the image of the Christian life — between the waters of Baptism and the promised land of heaven, we are tested and formed, fed by the true manna of the Eucharist and guided by God's presence.

**🖼️ Image hint:** An illustrated journey map on warm aged parchment: the route drawn as a soft golden line — starting at the pyramids, crossing the Red Sea, down to Mount Sinai labeled "1 year — covenant and Tabernacle," then a gentle tangle of looping lines circling Kadesh-barnea labeled "the long wilderness wandering," then a clean line north along the Jordan to Mount Nebo marked "end of the journey" — small tent icons, a guiding pillar of cloud, a compass rose — clear and easy to follow at a glance.

## Slide 14 — The Death of Moses: Final Days and the Promise of a Prophet Like Him *(optional — summarize in one sentence if short on time)* (40–41 min)

- Moses sees the Promised Land from Mount Nebo, but does not enter it (Deut 34:1-5)
- "Since then no prophet has arisen in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face" (Deut 34:10)
- Moses dies outside the land, leaving the promise open — the early Church read this promise as fulfilled in Christ: "God has raised up Jesus, of whom Moses wrote" (cf. Deut 18:15; Acts 3:22-23)
- The promise of a prophet like Moses: "A prophet like me will the LORD, your God, raise up for you" (Deut 18:15) — the theme of our coming classes

**🖼️ Image hint:** A serene, moving scene: elderly Moses at the cliff of Mount Nebo, cloak stirring in a gentle wind, gazing across the Jordan valley toward the Promised Land glowing in soft sunset light, rivers gleaming below — peaceful and hopeful, a bird soaring in the distance.

## Slide 15 — Moses as a Figure of Christ — and the Promise of a Changed Heart (41–44 min)

*(The comparison table is optional — if short on time, highlight one or two rows and move to the promise of the changed heart.)*

| Moses | Jesus |
| --- | --- |
| Saved from death as an infant | Preserved from Herod's attempt to kill him (Mt 2:13-15) |
| Freed Israel from slavery in Egypt | Frees us from slavery to sin |
| Gave the Law at Sinai | Gives the new law of love (Mt 5–7) |
| Interceded for the people | Is our one mediator (1 Tm 2:5) |
| The Passover lamb's blood saved Israel | Christ is our Passover lamb (1 Cor 5:7) |
| A servant in God's house | The Son over God's house (Heb 3:1-6) |

**Moses' promise of a changed heart (Deut 30:6):**

> "The LORD, your God, will circumcise your hearts and the hearts of your descendants, that you may love the LORD, your God with all your heart and all your being, in order that you may live." — Deuteronomy 30:6

- The history of Israel shows that the Law, though holy and good, could not by itself transform sinful hearts — Israel kept falling, from the golden calf through the wilderness to the rise and fall of the kingdom
- So God promised through Moses to do the inner work **himself** — a "circumcision of the heart," hearts changed from within
- Fulfilled in Christ: the new covenant written not on stone but on hearts (Jer 31:33), and the Holy Spirit giving "a new heart and a new spirit" at Baptism (Ezk 36:26); the Mosaic Law prepared for Christ (cf. CCC 1963-1964)
- We need more than a law — **we need a Savior**

**Takeaway:** Moses' life, ministry, and writings point beyond themselves toward Christ — liberator, lawgiver, and intercessor whose role anticipates Christ's.

**🖼️ Image hint:** A tender symbolic image: a heart of grey stone gently cracking open, warm golden light glowing from within, revealing a soft living heart of flesh inside — a white dove resting nearby, the stone tablets dimly visible in the background — transformation rendered quietly and beautifully.

## Slide 16 — What This Means for Us — and Closing (44–45 min)

**For us (OCIA candidates):**
- God calls **ordinary, imperfect people** — he can call and use you
- God takes the initiative: he called Moses by name, and he calls each of us by name in Baptism
- Like Israel, we pass "through the water," and then we walk the wilderness — a lifetime of being formed between Baptism and the promised land of heaven
- God still teaches his people to worship — in the Mass, Christ gathers his people into heavenly worship

**Key takeaways:**
- God formed Israel as his people through the Exodus, the covenant, and the Law (cf. CCC 62, 72)
- God took the initiative at the burning bush and revealed his name, "I AM WHO AM" (CCC 2575)
- The five books of Moses tell one story: promise, redemption, worship, journey, renewal
- God taught his people liturgy — the Tabernacle and the Ark — prefiguring Baptism, Eucharist, and Mary (CCC 1093-1095, 1340, 2676)
- Moses pointed toward the new heart God would give — fulfilled in Christ and the Spirit (Deut 30:6; cf. CCC 1963-1964)
